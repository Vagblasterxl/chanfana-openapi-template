# California Case Law — Post-Claim Retaliation & Adjuster Misconduct

The court decisions that control Ken's fact pattern: retaliation after a
workers' comp claim, and an adjuster lying about the claim. This is the case-law
layer on top of `CA-EMPLOYMENT-LAW.md` (statutes) — read that first.

> **NOT legal advice.** Case holdings get narrowed, distinguished, and
> overruled. An attorney must confirm each one still controls before relying on
> it. Verified against the sources listed below, 2026-07-08.

---

## The retaliation cases (§132a and beyond)

### Judson Steel Corp. v. WCAB (1978) — the §132a anchor
Worker got hurt, took benefits, came back — and was laid off two days later.
The California Supreme Court read §132a **broadly**: punishing a worker for
exercising workers' comp rights is discrimination, even where the employer
dresses it up as an ordinary business move. This is the case every §132a
petition stands on.

### Dept. of Rehabilitation v. WCAB (*Lauher*) (2003) — the limit
The Supreme Court pulled §132a back in: you must show **disparate treatment** —
that you were treated *worse than other employees* because of the industrial
injury, not just that something bad happened after your claim. An employer can
still make real business decisions (no forced re-hiring into jobs that don't
exist). **Practical meaning for the case file:** collect proof of how
*non-injured* coworkers were treated in the same situation — that comparison is
the whole ballgame after Lauher.

### City of Moorpark v. Superior Court (1998) 18 Cal.4th 1143 — the door to civil court
The big one. The Supreme Court held **§132a is NOT the exclusive remedy** for
discrimination based on a work injury. A worker whose injury left a disability
can ALSO sue in **regular civil court** under **FEHA** (disability
discrimination) and for **common-law wrongful termination** (*Tameny*). The
standards differ — losing a §132a petition doesn't doom the FEHA claim, and
winning one doesn't prove it. **Practical meaning:** the WCAB's capped,
no-jury remedies are not the ceiling. The same firing can support a §132a
petition (1-year WCAB clock) *and* a FEHA/CRD case (3-year clock) *and* a
Tameny claim (2-year clock) — three tracks, three separate deadlines, all in
the deadlines table.

## The adjuster cases — when the carrier can be reached in civil court

### Unruh v. Truck Insurance Exchange (1972) 7 Cal.3d 616 — the intentional-tort exception
A carrier's investigator deceived and romanced an injured claimant to stage
surveillance of her. The Supreme Court held: for **negligence**, the WCAB has
exclusive jurisdiction — but when the carrier commits an **intentional tort**
during claim handling, it steps outside its role as a comp carrier and becomes
a **"person other than the employer"** who CAN be sued in civil court for
damages. **Practical meaning:** garden-variety stinginess, delay, and hardball
stay locked inside the WCAB — but deceit-type intentional misconduct by the
adjuster/investigator is the crack in the exclusivity wall. Whether specific
lies rise to that level is exactly the question for an attorney.

### Vacanti v. State Comp. Ins. Fund (2001) 24 Cal.4th 800 — the modern test
The Supreme Court's framework for which claims against insurers survive
exclusivity: if the alleged misconduct is part of the normal **claims-handling
bargain** (even done badly, even done in bad faith), it stays at the WCAB; only
conduct outside that role supports a civil suit. It barred some claims and let
others through. **Practical meaning:** this is the case a defense lawyer will
throw at any civil complaint against the carrier — an attorney has to shape any
Unruh-type claim to survive Vacanti.

## How the tracks stack for a post-claim retaliation + lying-adjuster case

| Track | Where | Clock | What it gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| §132a petition (*Judson Steel*, watch *Lauher*) | WCAB | **1 yr** | Up to $10k added comp, reinstatement, lost wages |
| FEHA disability discrimination (*Moorpark*) | CRD → civil court | 3 yrs to CRD, then 1 yr | Full civil damages, jury |
| Wrongful termination (*Tameny*, via *Moorpark*) | Civil court | 2 yrs | Tort damages |
| Adjuster intentional misconduct (*Unruh*, shaped around *Vacanti*) | Civil court | Varies — attorney call | Damages against carrier/adjuster |
| Fraud referral (Ins. Code §1871.4) | DA / DOI Fraud Division | n/a — report it | Criminal accountability |
| Delay penalty (Lab. Code §5814) | WCAB | 2 yrs from missed payment | +25% or $10k on delayed benefits |

The 1-year §132a clock is still the shortest fuse. *Moorpark* is why missing it
wouldn't end everything — but nothing here should be allowed to lapse.
